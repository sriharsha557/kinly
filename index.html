The problem

Tracking was never the problem.

Everyone already knows what they should do: exercise, read, save, learn. Habit apps hand you a checkbox for it, and most people still abandon them within weeks. That's because a checkbox never touches the actual problem, which is staying consistent once motivation runs out.

Motivation fades faster than the goal takes to reach.
Willpower alone rarely survives the whole stretch.
When you quietly stop, nobody around you notices.
A solo streak breaks once, and the app gets deleted.

What it is

A private circle, built for accountability that actually feels good.

Pick the friends who'll notice if you go quiet. Set goals across whatever you're working on — health, wealth, ideas, learning, relationships — and log progress in one tap. Everyone in the circle sees each other's momentum, not a highlight reel.

The signature mechanic

Every goal plants something.

Kinly turns consistency into something you can actually see. Every day you log a goal your plant grows a little, not only when it reaches the next stage, and it never shrinks back. Go quiet and it wilts. No lectures, just a plant that notices.

Your whole circle stands in one row, each plant swaying to its own rhythm, under a sky that tracks your time of day from dawn through night.

Why a circle

What actually changes.

Most habit trackers are built for an audience of one. Kinly assumes the reason you stop isn't a missing feature, it's that nobody's watching.

Solo habit trackers

  • You track alone, so nobody sees when you quit.
  • Your own willpower is the only thing keeping you going.
  • One broken streak and the story just ends there.
  • Charts pile up, but nobody's actually encouraging you.

Kinly

  • Friends see your consistency, and your quiet weeks too.
  • Not wanting to let the circle down keeps you going.
  • A friend nudges you back after a bad week.
  • Opening the app leaves you feeling better, not worse.

Private by design

Built like a trusted room.

Kinly isn't a social network with a smaller radius. It's closer to a group chat: what happens in a circle stays in that circle, and nothing about it is designed to be public.

Goals, activity, and gardens are scoped to a single circle.
Invite codes are owner-controlled, and new joins need approval.
No followers, no algorithm, and no ads anywhere in the app.
Keep separate circles for family, college friends, or coworkers.
