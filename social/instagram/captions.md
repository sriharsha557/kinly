# Instagram captions — one per card

Posting order below tells a story arc across ~2 weeks (problem → philosophy → product), but each stands alone. Hashtag block at the bottom; use 5–10 per post, rotate them.

## 01 — Why the name (01-why-the-name.png)

kin: the people closest to you.
kindly: how they hold you to it.

That's the whole naming brief. We didn't want a word that sounded like productivity software, because Kinly isn't for optimizing yourself. It's for the small group of people who notice when you go quiet — and who you'd notice going quiet too.

Grow together. 🌱

## 02 — Why this logo (02-the-logo.png)

Two people. Look closer — their arms link into a loop with no end.

You keep them going. They keep you going. Nobody carries a goal alone here, and the loop only works if both people hold on.

That's the whole app in one mark.

## 03 — Not a habit tracker (03-not-a-habit-tracker.png)

Let's be clear about what Kinly is not.

A habit tracker gives you a checkbox. You already had a checkbox — it's called a notes app, and it lasted eleven days.

The checkbox was never the hard part. Staying consistent when nobody would notice you stopping — that's the hard part. So we built the noticing.

## 04 — The problem (04-the-problem.png)

Exercise. Read. Save. Learn.

You don't need another app to tell you this. You've known for years. Every January proves it.

Knowing was never the problem. Staying consistent after motivation runs out is the problem — and no amount of tracking fixes it, because tracking only talks to you.

## 05 — Nobody's watching (05-nobody-watching.png)

Here's why most people abandon habit apps within weeks:

quitting is silent.

You stop logging, and… nothing happens. No one asks where you went. The app sends a push notification you swipe away. It never feels like a decision, so it never feels like quitting.

Kinly's bet: the fix isn't a better streak screen. It's 2–10 friends who see your quiet week and send a nudge.

## 06 — Built like a trusted room (06-trusted-room.png)

Kinly isn't a social network with a smaller radius.

No public profiles. No followers. No strangers grading your progress. What happens in your circle stays in your circle — closer to a group chat than a feed.

You can't be honest about your goals in front of an audience. You can be honest in a trusted room.

## 07 — The garden (07-garden.png)

Every goal plants something. 🌱

Log progress, and your garden grows. Go quiet, and it wilts. No red badges, no guilt-trip notifications, no streak screaming at you in caps.

Just a plant that notices. And friends who water it with you.

## 08 — One loop, every day (08-the-loop.png)

The whole app, honestly:

Start a circle with 2–10 friends you trust. Set goals that actually matter to you. Log progress — a minute or two, most days. Watch streaks and gardens grow. And when someone in your circle goes quiet? Show up for them.

That's it. One loop, every day. The loop is the product.

## 09 — Quiet weeks (09-quiet-weeks.png)

Your friends see your streaks. And your quiet weeks too.

That sounds scary until you realize it's the entire point. Not pressure. Not shame. Gentle visibility — people who notice, and a one-tap nudge that says "hey, still here, still rooting for you."

Solo trackers are built for an audience of one. That's exactly why they fail.

## 10 — The whole network (10-whole-network.png)

2–10 friends. That's the whole network.

No algorithm deciding who sees you. No engagement metrics. No strangers to perform for. Your circle is the entire audience — which means you can chase real goals instead of impressive-looking ones.

Small on purpose. 🧡

# Run 2 — what's new (10 posts, 3–12 Aug 2026)

The first ten cards argued *why* Kinly exists. These ten show *what shipped*.
One a day, in this order — it opens on the garden (the thing people remember)
and closes on the quietest, most personal feature.

## 11 — It grows the moment you check in (11-garden-grows.png) · Mon 3 Aug

Your garden used to change three times. Day 3, day 14, day 30.

Which meant that on day 8, after eight straight days of showing up, it looked exactly like it did on day 3. Eight check-ins, nothing to show for them.

That's fixed. Log a goal now and your plant is taller before your thumb leaves the screen. Every single day moves it, and it never moves backwards.

Small thing. It changes how the whole app feels. 🌱

## 12 — Your garden keeps your hours (12-sky-knows-the-hour.png) · Tue 4 Aug

New: the sky over your circle's garden follows the actual time of day.

Warm rose at dawn. Clear at midday. Amber through the evening. Deep blue at night.

Checking in before work now looks nothing like checking in before bed — and after a week you stop noticing it consciously and just feel it. That's the idea.

## 13 — Three moods, one app (13-three-themes.png) · Wed 5 Aug

Dusk. Garden. Ember. Each in light or dark.

One accent colour at a time, everywhere — buttons, progress, the one thing on screen you're meant to tap. Everything else stays a warm neutral.

We had a version with a different colour per goal category. It looked like a highlighter exploded. An app that shouts in six colours isn't a calm one, so we took five away.

## 14 — Not every day is a goal day (14-light-moments.png) · Thu 6 Aug

Light Moments: two-minute games for your circle.

Would You Rather. Guess Who. A daily prompt nobody has to prepare for.

Because a group chat that only ever talks about goals dies in about nine days. The trivial stuff isn't a distraction from accountability — it's the thing that keeps the room warm enough for accountability to happen.

## 15 — Answer first, then you read (15-together.png) · Fri 7 Aug

One prompt a day for the whole circle. Everyone answers blind. Then it opens.

You can't see anyone's answer until you've written your own — so you can't read the room, can't hedge, can't quietly adjust yourself to match the group.

Turns out you find out more about people that way.

## 16 — Written by people, not a model (16-written-by-people.png) · Sat 8 Aug

We took the AI out of Kinly.

Every nudge your circle can send is a line a person wrote and we chose, one by one. Every circle prompt too.

Encouragement generated on demand isn't encouragement — it's autocomplete wearing a friend's voice. If a message is going to say "I noticed you went quiet," a human should have meant it.

Fewer moving parts. Nothing phoning home. 🧡

## 17 — A walk counts without you logging it (17-steps-count-themselves.png) · Sun 9 Aug

Connect Health Connect and your step goal fills itself in.

You go for the walk. The goal updates. Your plant grows. Your circle sees it.

The walk was always the point. Opening an app afterwards to tell it what your legs did was never the point.

## 18 — One a day, that's the whole plan (18-one-digest.png) · Mon 10 Aug

Kinly sends you one notification a day.

Everything your circle did — check-ins, streaks, nudges, someone finishing a goal — gathered into a single quiet digest.

Every other app on your phone is competing for your attention. We'd rather have a small amount of it and give it back. An app that pings you eleven times a day isn't helping you focus on anything.

## 19 — Ten seconds, no paragraph required (19-ten-seconds.png) · Tue 11 Aug

"How's today going?"

Tap a face. Done. Your circle can see it and reach out if it's a rough one.

No streak on it. No score. Nothing to keep up. Some days the most useful thing you can tell your friends takes ten seconds and no words at all.

## 20 — Write to the you who kept going (20-future-letters.png) · Wed 12 Aug

Future Letters: write to yourself, set the date it opens.

Nobody in your circle can read it. Not now, not ever. It's the one thing in Kinly that isn't shared.

Because some of what keeps you going is for the group — and some of it is a note from the version of you who started, to the version who's still here.
