# Instagram captions — one per card

Posting order below tells a story arc across ~2 weeks (problem → philosophy → product), but each stands alone. Hashtag block at the bottom; use 5–10 per post, rotate them.

## 01 — Why the name (01-why-the-name.png)

kin: the people closest to you.
kindly: how they hold you to it.

That's the whole naming brief. We didn't want a word that sounded like productivity software, because Kinly isn't for optimizing yourself. It's for the small group of people who notice when you go quiet — and who you'd notice going quiet too.

Grow together. 🌱

## 02 — Why this logo (02-the-logo.png)

Two people. Look closer — their arms link into a loop with no end.

You keep them going. They keep you going. Nobody carries a goal alone here, and the loop only works if both people hold on.

That's the whole app in one mark.

## 03 — Not a habit tracker (03-not-a-habit-tracker.png)

Let's be clear about what Kinly is not.

A habit tracker gives you a checkbox. You already had a checkbox — it's called a notes app, and it lasted eleven days.

The checkbox was never the hard part. Staying consistent when nobody would notice you stopping — that's the hard part. So we built the noticing.

## 04 — The problem (04-the-problem.png)

Exercise. Read. Save. Learn.

You don't need another app to tell you this. You've known for years. Every January proves it.

Knowing was never the problem. Staying consistent after motivation runs out is the problem — and no amount of tracking fixes it, because tracking only talks to you.

## 05 — Nobody's watching (05-nobody-watching.png)

Here's why most people abandon habit apps within weeks:

quitting is silent.

You stop logging, and… nothing happens. No one asks where you went. The app sends a push notification you swipe away. It never feels like a decision, so it never feels like quitting.

Kinly's bet: the fix isn't a better streak screen. It's 2–10 friends who see your quiet week and send a nudge.

## 06 — Built like a trusted room (06-trusted-room.png)

Kinly isn't a social network with a smaller radius.

No public profiles. No followers. No strangers grading your progress. What happens in your circle stays in your circle — closer to a group chat than a feed.

You can't be honest about your goals in front of an audience. You can be honest in a trusted room.

## 07 — The garden (07-garden.png)

Every goal plants something. 🌱

Log progress, and your garden grows. Go quiet, and it wilts. No red badges, no guilt-trip notifications, no streak screaming at you in caps.

Just a plant that notices. And friends who water it with you.

## 08 — One loop, every day (08-the-loop.png)

The whole app, honestly:

Start a circle with 2–10 friends you trust. Set goals that actually matter to you. Log progress — a minute or two, most days. Watch streaks and gardens grow. And when someone in your circle goes quiet? Show up for them.

That's it. One loop, every day. The loop is the product.

## 09 — Quiet weeks (09-quiet-weeks.png)

Your friends see your streaks. And your quiet weeks too.

That sounds scary until you realize it's the entire point. Not pressure. Not shame. Gentle visibility — people who notice, and a one-tap nudge that says "hey, still here, still rooting for you."

Solo trackers are built for an audience of one. That's exactly why they fail.

## 10 — The whole network (10-whole-network.png)

2–10 friends. That's the whole network.

No algorithm deciding who sees you. No engagement metrics. No strangers to perform for. Your circle is the entire audience — which means you can chase real goals instead of impressive-looking ones.

Small on purpose. 🧡
